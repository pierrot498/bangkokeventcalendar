Bangkok live music venues cover everything from a candlelit jazz trio in a hotel bar to 50,000 people singing along in a national stadium. On any given night you can catch blues near Victory Monument, a Thai indie band in a converted shophouse, or a K-pop tour stop out at Muang Thong Thani. The scene also changes quickly: 2026 saw some much-loved small venues close and new ones open.

This guide sorts the city's stages by type, with where they are, how to get there and what to expect. For tonight's gigs and upcoming tours, see our [music events](/events/category/music) and [nightlife listings](/events/category/nightlife).

## Jazz and blues bars

### Saxophone Pub (Victory Monument)

A Bangkok institution for well over two decades, Saxophone sits on **Phaya Thai Road**, a short walk from **BTS Victory Monument**. A different band plays most nights, mixing jazz, blues, funk and reggae across two floors. It opens in the early evening and runs until about 2:00. Weekends get packed, so arrive before the main set or book a table. Food and drinks are reasonably priced for a live venue.

### Brown Sugar (Chinatown)

Brown Sugar has been around since the mid-1980s and has moved several times, from Sarasin Road to Phra Sumen and now to **Soi Nana in Chinatown** (the old-town Soi Nana, not the Sukhumvit one). It typically opens around 17:00, closes on **Mondays**, and often has an acoustic set early in the evening followed by a full band later. The closest station is **MRT Hua Lamphong** or **MRT Wat Mangkon**.

### Bamboo Bar (Mandarin Oriental)

For the classic, dress-up jazz night, the **Bamboo Bar** at the Mandarin Oriental on the riverside is the most famous room in town. Expect polished resident and visiting musicians, cocktails at luxury-hotel prices, and a smart dress code. **Reservations are wise on weekends.** Take the Chao Phraya Express to **Oriental pier** or BTS to **Saphan Taksin** and walk or take the hotel shuttle boat.

### Other jazz spots worth knowing

- **Foojohn Jazz Club** in Talat Noi, a small, intimate room in the old riverside quarter.
- **Hotel bars** along Sukhumvit and the river often host weekly jazz nights; check their pages for the current line-up.

## Indie, alternative and new stages

### Marshall Livehouse (Charoen Krung)

Opened by the amp brand in **August 2025**, Marshall Livehouse is a five-storey venue in the **Charoen Krung** creative district, with a live stage, rehearsal studios and community space. It runs regular gigs and has quickly become a hub for local bands. It sits within reach of **BTS Saphan Taksin** and the riverside piers.

### Blueprint Livehouse (Ratchaprasong)

A dedicated gig venue that books Thai and international acts weekly, now based in the **Ratchaprasong** area near **BTS Chit Lom**. Tickets usually go through **Ticketmelon**, and many shows are standing room.

### Small bars and underground nights

Thong Lo and Ekkamai have long hosted smaller rooms for indie, reggae and electronic acts, and many cafes and bars in Ari, Charoen Krung and the old town run one-off live nights. These come and go, so follow venues on Instagram and check [our calendar](/calendar) before heading out.

### 2026 closures to note

Two well-known names closed this year, so ignore older guides that still list them:

- **Studio Lam** on Sukhumvit Soi 51, the home of Thai molam and world music, closed on **28 February 2026** after 12 years, following a long farewell run of nightly shows.
- **Noise House Lat Phrao** closed at the end of **March 2026**.

## Thai rock, pubs and big bar shows

A huge part of Bangkok's music life happens in Thai-style live pubs, where cover bands and well-known Thai acts play to tables of friends sharing bottles and snacks. These are found in Ratchada, Lat Phrao, Ramkhamhaeng and Rama 3. Big beer-hall venues book famous Thai singers on weekends, and the atmosphere is loud, joyful and very local. Sharing a table is normal, and ordering a bottle set for the group is the usual way to drink.

For heavier sounds, long-running rock bars near Phaya Thai and Ratchathewi play classic rock and metal most nights. Opening hours and line-ups change often, so check the venue's page before going.

## Big concert venues

### IMPACT Arena and Thunder Dome (Muang Thong Thani)

Most international arena tours stop at **IMPACT** in Muang Thong Thani, north of the city. The complex includes **IMPACT Arena** (the big indoor hall) and the smaller **Thunder Dome**, which in 2026 hosted rock and K-pop shows such as Avenged Sevenfold in October. The **MRT Pink Line** serves the area. Leave time: taxi queues after a sold-out show can be very long, so consider waiting it out over food.

### Rajamangala National Stadium

Bangkok's largest stadium, in the **Ramkhamhaeng / Hua Mak** area, is used for the biggest stadium tours and Thai mega-concerts. The **Airport Rail Link Ramkhamhaeng** station is the nearest rail link. Expect long walks, heavy security and huge crowds leaving at once.

### City-centre halls

- **UOB Live at Emsphere** on Sukhumvit, near **BTS Phrom Phong**, a mid-size hall for international and Thai acts.
- **Siam Pavalai Royal Grand Theatre** at Siam Paragon, near **BTS Siam**, used for concerts and theatre.
- **Lumpini Park and other parks** host free outdoor seasons, such as the Royal Bangkok Symphony Orchestra's Sunday concerts in the cool season.

## Practical tips for live music in Bangkok

- **Tickets:** big shows usually sell through **Thai Ticket Major** or **Ticketmelon**. Buy from official channels; resale scams are common for K-pop and stadium tours.
- **Cover charges:** many bars have no entry fee but expect you to order; some add a small cover or a minimum spend on headline nights.
- **Timing:** bands at bars often start around 20:00 to 21:00, with the main act after 22:00.
- **ID:** the legal drinking age is 20 and some venues check passports or ID.
- **Dress:** casual is fine almost everywhere except hotel bars like Bamboo Bar.
- **Alcohol sale hours and holidays:** some Buddhist holidays and election days mean no alcohol sales, and some venues close. Check before planning a night out.
- **Getting home:** the BTS and MRT stop around midnight; use metered taxis or ride-hailing apps after that.

## FAQ

### What is the best jazz bar in Bangkok?

It depends on the mood. Saxophone Pub is the lively, affordable classic; Bamboo Bar is the elegant hotel option; Brown Sugar offers a laid-back Chinatown setting.

### Is Studio Lam still open?

No. Studio Lam on Sukhumvit Soi 51 closed on 28 February 2026 after 12 years.

### How do I get to IMPACT Arena?

IMPACT is in Muang Thong Thani, north of central Bangkok. The MRT Pink Line serves the area, or take a taxi. Allow extra time after shows, when taxi queues are long.

### Where can I buy concert tickets in Bangkok?

Most big shows sell through Thai Ticket Major or Ticketmelon, plus the promoter's official site. Avoid unofficial resellers on social media.

### Do Bangkok live music bars charge entry?

Most bars do not, but they expect you to order drinks. Some charge a cover or minimum spend for headline acts or special nights.

### What time do live bands start in Bangkok?

Usually from about 20:00 or 21:00 at bars, with the main act later in the night. Concerts at arenas often start around 19:00 or 20:00.
