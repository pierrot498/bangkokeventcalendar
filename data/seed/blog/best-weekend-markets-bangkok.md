The best weekend markets in Bangkok are half the reason people fall for this city. You can spend a Saturday morning lost in Chatuchak, eat your way through a night market after dark, and be on a canal boat at a floating market by Sunday breakfast. The catch is that Bangkok's market scene moves fast. Leases expire, sites get redeveloped, and a market everyone recommended two years ago may now be a construction site.

This guide covers what is actually running in **2026**, where each market is, how to get there on the BTS and MRT, and when to go. For dated pop-ups, craft fairs and one-off markets, check our [markets events page](/events/category/markets) or the [full calendar](/calendar).

## Chatuchak Weekend Market

Chatuchak (often shortened to JJ) is still the big one: thousands of stalls selling clothes, homeware, ceramics, vintage, plants, pet supplies, art and a lot of food.

- **When:** the full market runs **Saturday and Sunday, roughly 9:00 to 18:00**. A smaller Friday evening session exists and leans towards wholesale buyers, so many souvenir and food stalls are closed then.
- **Plant market:** on **Wednesday and Thursday**, the plant and flower section opens from early morning, good if you like orchids and bonsai.
- **Getting there:** **MRT Kamphaeng Phet** (exit 2 drops you at the market's edge), **MRT Chatuchak Park** or **BTS Mo Chit**.

### How to do Chatuchak well

Arrive before 10:00. By midday the covered lanes get hot and crowded, and the best vintage stalls have been picked over. Grab a paper map at the information point or screenshot one, because the sections are numbered and easy to lose. Food is cheap: most snacks and drinks sit **around 40 to 100 THB**. Bargaining is fine on clothes and souvenirs if you stay friendly, less so on food.

## Or Tor Kor Market

Right across the road from Chatuchak, Or Tor Kor is Bangkok's upmarket fresh market. Think perfect mangoes, durian in season, curry pastes, dried seafood and ready-to-eat dishes in a clean, covered hall. It is not cheap by market standards, but quality is high.

- **When:** daily, roughly **6:00 to 18:00**, with the full range of stalls running from mid-morning. Weekends are packed.
- **Getting there:** **MRT Kamphaeng Phet exit 3** leads straight in.
- **Tip:** combine it with Chatuchak. Eat lunch at Or Tor Kor's food court, then shop.

## Night markets that are open in 2026

### Jodd Fairs Ratchada

The original **Jodd Fairs Rama 9 closed in January 2025** when its lease ended, so ignore older blog posts sending you there. The market now runs on **Ratchadaphisek Road next to Big C Place**, a few minutes' walk from **MRT Thailand Cultural Centre**. It opens daily in the evening, roughly 17:00 to midnight or later. Expect rows of food stalls (the giant "volcano" pork ribs are still the photo everyone takes), drinks, and some clothing.

The separate DanNeramit site on Phahonyothin Road has changed operators and names since 2025, so do not plan a trip around it without checking its current page.

### Train Night Market Ratchada is back

Good news for regulars: the **Train Night Market Ratchada reopened on 27 March 2026** on its old plot behind Esplanade Ratchada, after years of closure and a spell as The One Ratchada. It is again known for its photogenic rows of coloured tents seen from the mall car park. Time Out reported it running **daily from about 17:00 to 1:00**. It is a short walk from **MRT Thailand Cultural Centre** (exit 3), so you can do it and Jodd Fairs in one evening.

### Train Night Market Srinakarin

The original Rot Fai market behind **Seacon Square** on Srinakarin Road is still the place for vintage furniture, old signs, vinyl and retro collectibles, plus a big food zone and bars. It runs **Thursday to Sunday evenings**, with the full spread on weekends. It is far from central Bangkok; the MRT Yellow Line along Srinakarin Road gets you closer, then a short taxi or motorbike taxi.

## Creative and neighbourhood markets

### Chang Chui, Thonburi

Chang Chui is a creative park on **Sirindhorn Road in Bang Phlat**, built around a decommissioned airliner, with small shops, bars, art and evening events. Listings show it open **daily from around 11:00 to 23:00**, but individual shops keep their own hours, so evenings are the best bet. It is a taxi ride from the MRT Blue Line on the Thonburi side.

### Talad Noi and the Talakkia Friendly Market

Talad Noi is the riverside pocket of Chinatown known for old shophouses, mechanic workshops and street art. Separately, the **Talakkia Friendly Market** pops up near Phanurangsi pier, typically on the **first weekend of the month**, with local food, crafts and riverside events. Dates move, so check its social pages. Walk from **MRT Hua Lamphong** or take a boat to the Marine Department pier. ## Floating markets

### Inside Bangkok: Khlong Lat Mayom and Taling Chan

If you want a canal market without a long day trip, head to the Taling Chan area on the Thonburi side. **Khlong Lat Mayom** runs on **weekends, roughly 8:00 to 17:00**, and is more local than touristy, with most dishes **under about 80 THB** and short boat rides along the canals. **Taling Chan Floating Market** nearby is also a weekend affair, with food served from boats moored to a floating platform. A taxi from **BTS Talat Phlu** or **BTS Bang Wa** is the easiest route.

### Day trips: Damnoen Saduak and Amphawa

**Damnoen Saduak**, around 100 km southwest, is the famous postcard market. It is very touristy now, so go early (before 9:00) to see it at its most authentic, and agree boat prices before you board. **Amphawa** is livelier in the late afternoon and evening and is mainly a **Friday to Sunday** market, popular with Thai weekenders. Both are easiest with a tour or a hired car.

## Practical tips for Bangkok markets

- **Carry cash in small notes.** Many stalls take Thai QR payments, but tourists often cannot use them; 20 and 100 THB notes make life easy.
- **Go early or go late.** Day markets are best before 10:00; night markets get going after 19:00.
- **Use the train, not a taxi,** for Chatuchak and Ratchada on weekends. Traffic around both is slow.
- **Bring a tote bag.** Plastic bags are less common now, and you will buy more than planned.
- **Watch the rain.** In the wet season (roughly June to October) open-air night markets can pause for heavy showers. Have a backup covered market.
- **Durian and the MRT do not mix.** Station staff can refuse entry if you carry it.
- **Respect the 18:00 anthem** in public spaces and parks; people stop and stand briefly.
- **Bargain with a smile.** A small discount on clothes is normal; haggling over a 50 THB snack is not.

A classic weekend: Saturday morning at Chatuchak and Or Tor Kor, Saturday night at Jodd Fairs and Train Night Market Ratchada, Sunday morning at Khlong Lat Mayom. ## FAQ

### Is Chatuchak open on weekdays?

Only partly. The full market is a **Saturday and Sunday** affair. The plant section opens on Wednesday and Thursday, and there is a smaller, mostly wholesale Friday evening session.

### Is Jodd Fairs still at Rama 9?

No. The Rama 9 site closed in January 2025. Jodd Fairs now operates on Ratchadaphisek Road next to Big C Place, near MRT Thailand Cultural Centre.

### Has the Train Night Market Ratchada reopened?

Yes. It reopened on 27 March 2026 behind Esplanade Ratchada and was reported running daily in the evenings. The Srinakarin branch is also still operating, mainly Thursday to Sunday.

### What is the best floating market near central Bangkok?

For an easy half day, Khlong Lat Mayom or Taling Chan on the Thonburi side, both open on weekends. Damnoen Saduak and Amphawa are better as full day trips.

### How much money should I bring to a Bangkok market?

For food and drinks, 300 to 500 THB per person goes a long way. For shopping, bring what you plan to spend in cash, as card acceptance varies.

### Are Bangkok markets safe at night?

Generally yes. Keep your phone and wallet in a front pocket in crowded lanes, and use metered taxis or ride-hailing apps to get home late.
