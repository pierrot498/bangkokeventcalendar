New Year's Eve in Bangkok is one of the biggest nights in Southeast Asia. Hundreds of thousands of people gather for the free countdown at CentralWorld, fireworks light up the Chao Phraya from ICONSIAM, rooftop bars charge their highest prices of the year, and temples hold quiet midnight chanting for those who want a calmer start to 2027. This guide covers the main options for the **2026/27 countdown**, how to get there and what to expect.

In 2026, New Year's Eve falls on a **Thursday**, and **Friday 1 January 2027** is a public holiday, which turns it into a long weekend. Expect the city to be busy from Christmas onwards. Line-ups and programmes for 2026/27 had not been announced at the time of writing, so the details below are based on recent editions. Check each venue's official channels in December.

## CentralWorld countdown: the classic

The countdown at **CentralWorld** on Ratchadamri Road is Bangkok's answer to Times Square. A huge stage goes up in the plaza in front of the mall, nearby roads around Ratchaprasong are closed to traffic, and the night ends with fireworks over the skyline. The most recent edition drew more than **350,000 people**, according to the organiser, and added a drone show to the fireworks for the first time.

Entry to the street party is **free**. In recent years crowds started gathering from around 5 PM and performances began in the early evening, with Thai pop stars and bands building up to midnight.

### Getting there

Roads close from the afternoon, so do not plan on a taxi. Take the **BTS to Chit Lom or Siam** and follow the skywalk. Stations get extremely crowded close to midnight, and on recent New Year's Eves the BTS and MRT have run extended hours. Check the operators' announcements for 2026 before you go.

## ICONSIAM and the river fireworks

On the Thonburi side of the river, **ICONSIAM** has become the city's main fireworks venue. For the last countdown it ran a five-day festival in its River Park, with a long programme of Thai and international performers, finishing with a midnight display fired along more than a kilometre of the Chao Phraya. Reports said it could be seen from several kilometres away.

If you want to watch from ICONSIAM itself, arrive early and expect security checks and closed-off areas. Take the **BTS Gold Line to Charoen Nakhon**, or a ferry from Sathorn Pier near **BTS Saphan Taksin**. Many people prefer to watch from across the water instead: riverside hotels, restaurants and bars along Charoen Krung and the Silom riverside sell New Year's packages with a view of the display.

Asiatique The Riverfront, a little further downstream, usually hosts its own countdown party and is another option with river views.

## Rooftop bars

Bangkok's rooftop bars are spectacular on New Year's Eve, but this is their most expensive night. Most popular rooftops switch to **ticketed entry or set packages**, and walk-ins are rare. Well-known options include **Sky Bar at lebua** in Silom, **Vertigo at the Banyan Tree** in Sathorn, and **Octave** at the Marriott in Thong Lo, plus the open-air **Mahanakhon SkyWalk**, which typically runs its own countdown event. Prices change every year and range widely, from a few thousand baht per person for drinks packages to much more for dinner and a view of the fireworks.

A few tips: book in early December or before, read what is included (free-flow drinks, dinner, a seat or just entry), and check the dress code. Smart casual is standard, and shorts and flip-flops are usually refused. For more nights out, browse [nightlife events](/events/category/nightlife).

## River cruises

A dinner cruise on the Chao Phraya puts you right in front of the fireworks without the crush of the crowds. Operators leave from piers such as ICONSIAM, Asiatique and the big riverside hotels, and New Year's Eve prices are much higher than on a normal night, often several thousand baht per person. Cruises are among the first things to sell out. Book through the operator or a reputable agent, and confirm the boarding time, return time and whether drinks are included.

## Temples: a quieter New Year

Many Thais start the year at a temple. A lot of temples hold **year-crossing chanting** ("suat mon kham pi") through midnight, where people chant, make merit and receive blessings. It is a peaceful, very local experience, and visitors are welcome if they are respectful. Some larger temples in the old town organise special events, while neighbourhood temples are calmer.

Dress modestly with shoulders and knees covered, take your shoes off where asked, and keep phones on silent. The morning of 1 January is also a popular time for making merit by offering food to monks.

## Practical tips for New Year's Eve in Bangkok

- **Use the BTS, MRT and river boats**. Roads around CentralWorld and the river close, and ride-hailing prices surge dramatically around midnight.
- **Plan your way home**. Decide in advance whether you will walk, wait for the trains to calm down or stay nearby.
- **Pick a meeting point**. Phone networks slow down in big crowds, so agree where to meet if you get separated.
- **Travel light**. Bags may be searched at venue entrances; leave large bags and anything valuable at the hotel.
- **Carry cash and water**. Street stalls are everywhere, but queues for card payments and toilets are long.
- **Book early**. Rooftops, cruises and hotel packages can sell out weeks before.
- **Mind fireworks rules**. Setting off your own fireworks in public is restricted in Bangkok, and sky lanterns are not allowed.

## FAQ

### Is the CentralWorld countdown free?

Yes. The main street party in front of CentralWorld is free to enter. Arrive by early evening if you want to be near the stage, and expect security checks.

### Where can I see fireworks in Bangkok on New Year's Eve?

The biggest display in recent years has been along the Chao Phraya at ICONSIAM, visible from much of the riverside. CentralWorld also ends its countdown with fireworks over Ratchaprasong.

### Do the BTS and MRT run late on New Year's Eve?

In recent years the operators have extended service hours on New Year's Eve. Exact times are announced in December, so check the BTS and MRT official channels before you go.

### How much does a rooftop bar cost on New Year's Eve?

Most well-known rooftops sell tickets or packages for the night, typically starting from a few thousand baht per person and rising for dinner packages. Book ahead and check what the price includes.

### Is 1 January a public holiday in Thailand?

Yes. New Year's Day is a public holiday, and in 2027 it falls on a Friday, so many people have a long weekend. Government offices and banks close, though most malls and attractions stay open.

### Is it safe to celebrate New Year's Eve in Bangkok?

Generally yes, with large police and security presence at the main venues. The main issues are crowd crushes and pickpockets, so keep your belongings close, stay aware and leave if an area feels too packed.
