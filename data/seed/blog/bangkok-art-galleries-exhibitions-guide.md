Bangkok's art scene has grown fast. In a few years the city has gained a major new private museum, a kunsthalle in a burnt-out printing house and a biennale that spreads across temples and malls. This guide to **Bangkok art galleries and exhibitions** covers the big institutions, the independent spaces worth seeking out, how to get there and what it costs, so you can plan a day of gallery hopping whether you live here or are visiting for a week.

## The big institutions

### Bangkok Art and Culture Centre (BACC)

The **BACC** is the obvious starting point: a large, white spiral building at the Pathum Wan intersection, connected by skywalk to **BTS National Stadium** and close to Siam. It hosts major rotating exhibitions on the upper floors, while the lower levels hold small galleries, bookshops, craft stores and cafes. **Entry to the main exhibitions is usually free**, and it is closed on Mondays. It is also one of the main venues of the Bangkok Art Biennale.

### Jim Thompson Art Center

Most visitors know the Jim Thompson House, but the **Jim Thompson Art Center** next door, on Soi Kasemsan 2 off Rama I Road, runs some of the most thoughtful exhibitions in the city. Shows are often research-driven, mixing contemporary art with craft and history. Its autumn 2026 group exhibition, *And Still, Another After*, is listed to run into **March 2027**, and entry has been free. It is a short walk from BTS National Stadium, so it pairs easily with the BACC.

### MOCA Bangkok

The **Museum of Contemporary Art (MOCA)** on Vibhavadi Rangsit Road in the north of the city houses a large private collection of modern and contemporary Thai art across several floors, including big names such as Thawan Duchanee and Chalermchai Kositpipat. It is out of the centre, so most people take a taxi or Grab. Recent listings put adult entry at around **300 THB**, with the museum closed on Mondays; check the official site before you go, as prices and hours have changed over the years.

### Dib Bangkok

**Dib Bangkok** is the city's newest major museum and the most talked-about. It **opened in December 2025** in a converted 1980s warehouse on **Sukhumvit Soi 40**, redesigned by architect Kulapat Yantrasast, and is billed as Thailand's first international contemporary art museum. Its debut exhibition, *(In)visible Presence*, ran until August 2026. The nearest BTS is **Phra Khanong**, about a 20 minute walk or a quick motorbike taxi.

Listings have shown it open **Thursday to Monday** with tickets around **700 THB for foreign visitors** and less for Thai residents, but details vary between sources, so **check the museum's website** for current hours, prices and the exhibition on view.

### Bangkok Kunsthalle

Set in the former Thai Watana Panich printing house near Chinatown, partly damaged by fire decades ago, **Bangkok Kunsthalle** is one of the most atmospheric art spaces in Asia. It mounts ambitious, often immersive shows. In autumn 2026 it presents *Bright at Midnight*, works by Dutch artist Bas Jan Ader, listed to run **until 10 January 2027**, with free entry and afternoon to evening opening hours. MRT Hua Lamphong and Wat Mangkon are both within walking distance.

## Galleries and independent spaces

### River City Bangkok

**River City Bangkok**, on the Chao Phraya next to Si Phraya pier, is an art and antiques centre with gallery spaces, auction rooms and antique dealers. It hosts rotating exhibitions, often with free entry, and events such as the Mango Art Festival in spring. Take the Chao Phraya Express Boat to Si Phraya or the free shuttle boat from Sathorn pier.

### 100 Tonson Foundation

**100 Tonson** is a non-profit space in an elegant building on Soi Tonson, off Ploenchit Road, close to **BTS Chit Lom**. It shows serious Thai and international contemporary art in calm, well-lit rooms. It has typically opened Thursday to Sunday.

### Nova Contemporary

**Nova Contemporary** is a commercial gallery with a strong eye for emerging Thai and Southeast Asian artists. Exhibitions change regularly and openings draw a young, art-world crowd. Check its social pages for the current location details and show.

### Speedy Grandma

A long-running alternative space in a converted shophouse in the **Charoenkrung** area, **Speedy Grandma** is known for experimental shows, small pop-ups and lively opening nights where the gallery spills out onto the soi. Hours are short and it is best to follow its Facebook page before visiting.

### Tentacles

**Tentacles** is an artist-run space with a cafe and shop, now located off **Narathiwas Soi 22** in the Sathorn and Yannawa area, sharing a warehouse compound with other galleries. It runs exhibitions, workshops and screenings, and is a relaxed stop on an afternoon gallery crawl.

### Charoenkrung and Talat Noi

The streets around **Charoenkrung Road and Talat Noi** hold dozens of small galleries, photo spaces, design shops and cafes in old shophouses. It is the easiest area to wander without a fixed plan. Start near Saphan Taksin or River City and work your way north on foot.

## Bangkok Art Biennale 2026

The fifth **Bangkok Art Biennale** runs from **29 October 2026 to 28 February 2027** under the theme **"Angels and Mara"**. As in past editions, works are spread across the city, from temples such as **Wat Arun, Wat Pho and Wat Prayoon** to the **BACC**, **Museum Siam** and shopping centres including **One Bangkok, centralwOrld and Siam Paragon**. Many venues are free, though some temples charge their usual entry fee. Pick up a map at a main venue and plan it by area: the riverside temples in one day, the Siam and Ratchaprasong malls in another.

## Gallery nights and openings

Bangkok has had several gallery-hopping events over the years, with groups of galleries in Charoenkrung, Silom and Sathorn opening late on the same evening, sometimes with shuttles between them. Dates are irregular, so watch gallery Instagram and Facebook pages. The simplest way to experience the scene is an **opening night**: most galleries hold free openings, usually early evening, with drinks and the artist present.

Many of these venues are free, and they make great additions to our list of [free things to do in Bangkok](/blog/free-things-to-do-in-bangkok).

## Practical tips

- **Check opening days.** Many galleries close on Monday, and some also on Tuesday or Wednesday.
- **Go late afternoon.** Several spaces open from midday or 2 pm and stay open into the evening, which beats the heat.
- **Group by area.** BACC and Jim Thompson Art Center pair well; so do Bangkok Kunsthalle, River City and Charoenkrung.
- **Carry some cash** for entry fees at private museums and for gallery cafes.
- **Dress for temples** if you are following the Biennale: shoulders and knees covered.
- **Ask before photographing.** Most spaces allow photos without flash, but some works are off limits.
- **Follow venues on Instagram** for opening nights and last-minute talks.

## FAQ

### What is the best art museum in Bangkok?

For a free, central first visit, the BACC. For international contemporary art, Dib Bangkok is the city's most ambitious new museum. For Thai modern masters, head to MOCA.

### When is the Bangkok Art Biennale 2026?

The fifth edition runs from 29 October 2026 to 28 February 2027 under the theme "Angels and Mara", at venues including Wat Arun, Wat Pho, BACC and several malls.

### Is Dib Bangkok open?

Yes. Dib Bangkok opened in December 2025 on Sukhumvit Soi 40. It has generally opened Thursday to Monday, but check its website for current hours and exhibitions.

### Are Bangkok galleries free?

Most commercial galleries and non-profit spaces are free, as are the BACC's main exhibitions and many Biennale venues. Private museums like Dib Bangkok and MOCA charge entry.

### Which area is best for gallery hopping?

Charoenkrung and Talat Noi have the densest mix of small galleries, plus River City and Bangkok Kunsthalle nearby. Siam is best for the big public institutions.
