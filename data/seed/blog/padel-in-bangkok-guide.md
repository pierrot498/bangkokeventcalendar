Padel in Bangkok has gone from a handful of rooftop courts to one of the city's busiest social sports in about three years. New clubs keep opening along the Sukhumvit line, evening slots fill up days ahead, and the Americano nights have become a proper way to meet people, especially for expats who just arrived. The good news: padel is easy to pick up. If you have ever held a tennis or squash racket, you will be rallying within an hour.

This guide covers where to play padel in Bangkok in 2026, what courts and lessons cost in baht, how the social and tournament scene works, and what you need to get started. ## What is padel, in one paragraph

Padel is a doubles racket sport played on a court about a third the size of a tennis court, enclosed by glass walls and mesh fencing. You serve underarm, score like tennis, and the ball can bounce off the walls, which keeps rallies long and forgiving. It is **almost always played as doubles**, which is why it works so well as a social sport.

## Best padel clubs in Bangkok

Clubs open, expand and rebrand quickly, so treat this as a starting map and check each club's booking page for current courts and hours. Most Bangkok clubs take bookings through apps like **Playtomic** or **MATCHi**, or their own booking system.

### Pad Thai Padel (Thonglor / Ekkamai)

On Soi Saen Sabai off Rama IV, a short ride from **BTS Thong Lo** or **BTS Ekkamai**. A modern club with indoor and outdoor courts, a clubhouse bar, a pro shop and a busy calendar of social events. It hosted an Asia Pacific Padel Tour stop, the Pad Thai Padel Grand Slam, in January 2026, so it is also one of the city's more competitive venues. Court hire has been listed **from around 1,400 THB per hour**.

### Kross Padel (On Nut, Phrom Phong, Asoke)

Kross runs several sites. The original club near **BTS On Nut** (Phra Khanong side) has courts next to a pool, ice bath and sauna, and runs morning yoga and "padel and drinks" socials. There is also a rooftop Sky Club in **Phrom Phong**, an indoor venue and a branch in **Asoke**. Published rates start **from around 800 THB per hour outdoors and 1,000 THB indoors** off-peak, rising to roughly 1,200 to 1,600 THB at peak times depending on the site.

### Baan Padel (Sukhumvit 67)

On Sukhumvit Soi 67, between **BTS Phra Khanong** and **BTS On Nut**. A larger club with around seven courts, including air-conditioned ones, plus equipment rental, a shop, a restaurant and free parking. It runs a regular programme of Americano, Mexicano and King of the Court nights on Playtomic.

### The Padel Co. (Sukhumvit 50)

Near **BTS On Nut**, sharing space with a basketball club. Indoor and air-conditioned, which matters a lot in April or during a rainy-season downpour. Listed **from around 1,400 THB per hour**.

### Sterling Sport & Wellness (Sukhumvit 24)

Near **BTS Phrom Phong**, with air-conditioned indoor courts. It hosted the Bangkok stop of the Tatler Padel Series in August 2026, and several independent coaches teach here.

### No Drama Padel (Charoen Nakhon, Thonburi side)

Four covered courts near **ICONSIAM**, easy from the **Gold Line** or by river. Its published prices are a useful benchmark: **900 THB per court per hour off-peak** (weekday daytime) and **1,200 THB at peak**, with racket rental at 150 THB.

### Others worth knowing

**Bangkok Padel** on the rooftop of the Ambassador Hotel, Sukhumvit 11 (**BTS Nana**), was one of the first clubs in the city and is known for Americano nights, but it has been reported as closed for renovation, so check before you go. **PlayerBox** near **MRT Sam Yan** combines padel with an arcade and games centre, and **Padel Club Bangkok** in Pathumwan has floodlit outdoor courts popular after work.

## How much does padel cost in Bangkok?

Court prices are **per court, not per player**, and you split them four ways. Rough 2026 ranges:

- **Off-peak court hire:** about 800 to 1,000 THB per hour (weekday daytime).
- **Peak court hire:** about 1,200 to 1,600 THB per hour (evenings and weekends).
- **A 90-minute game for four at peak:** typically 1,800 to 2,400 THB, so around **450 to 600 THB each**.
- **Racket rental:** usually 100 to 200 THB.
- **Americano or social nights:** commonly around 300 to 600 THB per player.
- **Private lessons:** roughly 1,400 to 2,900 THB per hour including court, depending on the coach and venue.
- **Group clinics:** often 750 to 1,500 THB per person.

Prices change often and promotions are common, so confirm on the booking app before you go.

## Coaching and how to start

If you are new, book **one private or semi-private lesson** first. After that, most people learn fastest simply by playing.

Most clubs run beginner clinics, ladies' sessions, kids' academies and level-based group classes. Several independent coaches also work across venues; some, like Padel Bangkok at Sterling, sell lesson packs that bring the per-session price down.

### Finding people to play with

- **Americanos** are the easiest way in: sign up solo, the club rotates partners every few games, and you meet a dozen players in two hours.
- **Playtomic open matches** let you join a game posted by others at your level.

## Tournaments and leagues

Bangkok's competitive scene is growing. Clubs run internal ladders, monthly Americano tournaments and corporate leagues, and the city now draws international events: the **Asia Pacific Padel Tour** stopped at Pad Thai Padel in January 2026, and the **Tatler Padel Series** came to Bangkok in August 2026. There is also a national circuit, the Thai Padel Series, with pro and amateur categories. Dates for the next season are announced late, so we add them to the [padel calendar](/sports/padel) as soon as they are confirmed.

## Gear: what you actually need

You do not need to buy anything for your first few sessions. When you are hooked:

- **Racket:** a round-shaped, softer racket is more forgiving for beginners. Expect roughly 3,000 to 8,000 THB for a decent entry-level model; top-end rackets cost much more.
- **Shoes:** padel or clay-court tennis shoes with a herringbone sole. Running shoes have poor side support on the artificial grass.
- **Balls:** padel balls (slightly lower pressure than tennis balls) come in tubes of three; clubs sell them.

## Practical tips for playing padel in Bangkok

- **Book early for evenings.** Peak slots from 6 PM to 10 PM go days in advance; weekday mornings are cheaper and easier.
- **Mind the heat.** Outdoor courts at midday from March to May are brutal. Choose indoor or air-conditioned courts, or play early or late.
- **Rainy season** (roughly June to October) means covered courts are worth paying extra for.
- **Take the BTS.** Most clubs are a short motorbike taxi or Grab ride from a Sukhumvit line station; parking can be tight.
- **Respect court time.** Sessions start and end on the hour or half hour; arrive ten minutes early to warm up.
- **Hydrate properly.** Add electrolytes for anything longer than 90 minutes.

## FAQ

### How much does it cost to play padel in Bangkok?

Court hire is typically around 800 to 1,000 THB per hour off-peak and 1,200 to 1,600 THB at peak, per court. Split between four players, a 90-minute evening game usually works out to roughly 450 to 600 THB each.

### Where is the best place to play padel in Bangkok?

Most clubs are along the Sukhumvit corridor: Pad Thai Padel near Thonglor and Ekkamai, Kross Padel and Baan Padel around On Nut and Phra Khanong, and Sterling near Phrom Phong. On the Thonburi side, No Drama Padel near ICONSIAM is a good option.

### Can beginners play padel in Bangkok?

Yes. Padel is one of the easiest racket sports to learn, and most clubs offer beginner clinics, private lessons and rental rackets. One lesson followed by an Americano night is a great way to start.

### Are there padel tournaments in Bangkok?

Yes. Clubs run regular Americano and Mexicano tournaments, and Bangkok has hosted international events such as the Asia Pacific Padel Tour and the Tatler Padel Series. Check our padel page for upcoming dates.

### Is padel played indoors in Bangkok?

Both. Several clubs have covered or air-conditioned indoor courts, which are the best option during the hot season and the rainy season. Outdoor courts are usually a little cheaper.
