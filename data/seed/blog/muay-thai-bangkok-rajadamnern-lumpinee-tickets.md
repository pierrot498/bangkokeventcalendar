Seeing **muay thai in Bangkok** live is one of the best nights out the city offers, whether you are a lifelong fight fan or a first-time visitor. The two famous names are **Rajadamnern Stadium** and **Lumpinee Stadium**, and between them there is a fight card almost every night of the week. This guide covers how the two stadiums differ, which nights to go, what tickets cost, how to buy them without getting overcharged, and where to train if watching makes you want to try it.

For upcoming cards and other combat sports events, check our [muay thai section](/sports/muay-thai) and the full [sports events listings](/events/category/sports).

## Rajadamnern vs Lumpinee: the two great stadiums

### Rajadamnern Stadium

Rajadamnern is **the oldest muay thai stadium in Thailand**. Construction started in the early 1940s and the stadium opened in **1945**, on Ratchadamnoen Nok Road in the old town, not far from Democracy Monument.

It is also the most tourist-friendly option, with **fights almost every night**. The weekly pattern in 2026 has typically looked like this:

- **Monday, Tuesday, Friday:** shorter "Knockout" cards, roughly 7 pm to 9 pm
- **Wednesday:** New Power, around 6 pm to 10 pm
- **Thursday:** Petchyindee, around 6 pm to 10 pm
- **Saturday:** Rajadamnern World Series (RWS), around 7 pm to 10 pm
- **Sunday:** Kiatpetch, around 6 pm to 10 pm

Promoters and start times change, and the stadium can close for public holidays or mourning periods, so **check the official Rajadamnern site for the current week** before you go.

**Getting there:** there is no station on the doorstep. The nearest is **MRT Sam Yot**, about a 20 to 25 minute walk, so most people take a taxi or Grab for the last stretch. From Khao San Road it is a short tuk-tuk or taxi ride.

### Lumpinee Stadium

Lumpinee opened in **1956** on Rama IV Road, next to Lumpini Park, and is run by the Royal Thai Army. In **2014 it moved** to a larger, modern building on **Ram Intra Road in Bang Khen**, in the north of the city. Older guides still list the original site.

Since 2023 Lumpinee has been closely tied to **ONE Championship**, which runs its **ONE Friday Fights** (often called ONE Lumpinee) cards there on most Fridays. Other promotions run on other nights, with Saturday cards also common, but the schedule is narrower than Rajadamnern's. Check the stadium's and ONE's pages for the week you are in town.

**Getting there:** the easiest rail option is the **MRT Pink Line to Ram Inthra 3**, then roughly a 10 to 15 minute walk. The BTS station at Wat Phra Sri Mahathat (Sukhumvit Line) is further away, so a short taxi from there makes sense. There is not much else nearby, so plan your ride home before the last fight.

## RWS and ONE Lumpinee: the modern shows

The two big modern events are the easiest for newcomers to enjoy.

### Rajadamnern World Series (RWS)

RWS is the Saturday show at Rajadamnern. It is built for an international audience and TV: **no gambling in the crowd**, production lighting, and shorter fights that are scored to reward action. It is busy and popular, so **RWS tickets can sell out**, especially for big title nights.

### ONE Friday Fights at Lumpinee

ONE's Friday cards are fast, televised and packed with top Thai and international fighters. Expect a long card that starts in the early evening (around 7:30 pm has been typical) with the biggest bouts later. Some cards also include MMA bouts alongside muay thai, so check the line-up if you only want traditional fights.

### Traditional cards

Weeknight and promoter cards (Petchyindee, Kiatpetch and others) are where you see muay thai as many Thais follow it: five rounds, live pi-chawa music, and a loud, gesturing betting crowd in the upper tiers. Less polished, more authentic, and usually cheaper.

## Ticket tiers and rough prices

Both stadiums sell three broad levels of seat. Prices vary by night and promoter, so treat these as **rough 2026 ranges**:

- **Third class / standing (upper tier):** around **1,000 to 1,500 THB**. Furthest from the ring, often with the gamblers. Lively, but the view is limited.
- **Second class / club seats:** around **1,500 to 2,500 THB**. A good middle ground.
- **Ringside / VIP:** roughly **2,000 to 4,500 THB** at Rajadamnern. Big ONE cards at Lumpinee have premium ringside and VIP seats that can go well past this.

Thai nationals sometimes pay less than foreigners; that is normal, not a scam.

## Buying tickets: official channels vs touts

**Buy from official sources.** That means:

1. **The stadium's own website or box office.** Rajadamnern's site links each night to its ticket seller, and Lumpinee sells at the venue.
2. **ONE Championship's official ticket partners** for Friday Fights.
3. **Well-known booking platforms** such as Klook or Pelago, if you value convenience. You pay a small premium, but the price is fixed.

**Avoid street touts**, hotel "tour desks" with vague packages, and people outside the stadium who insist the box office is closed. Touts often sell standard seats at ringside prices, or bundle overpriced taxi transfers. If someone approaches you near the entrance, walk past and go to the counter.

## Practical tips

- **Arrive 45 to 60 minutes early.** Some seats are first come, first served, and online bookings sometimes need to be exchanged for a wristband or ticket.
- **The main event comes late.** Early fights feature younger, less experienced fighters. If you only want the headliners, arriving an hour into the card is fine.
- **Bring cash** for drinks and snacks, even if you paid for your ticket online.
- **Dress lightly.** The stadiums get hot and humid, especially in the upper tiers.
- **Don't join the betting.** Gambling is part of the culture on some nights but is not for visitors, and it is legally grey.
- **Combine your night.** Rajadamnern is close to the old town and Khao San; after a fight there you have plenty of food and [live music options](/blog/bangkok-live-music-venues) nearby.

## Stadium etiquette

- **Watch the wai kru.** Before each fight, fighters perform the wai kru ram muay, a ritual dance honouring their teachers. Stay seated and quiet; it is a respectful moment.
- **Cheer, but don't heckle.** Loud support is welcome; insulting fighters is not.
- **Respect the mongkhon.** The headband fighters wear in the ring is sacred to them. Don't touch it if you meet fighters afterwards.
- **Don't block the view** by standing for videos in seated areas.

## Where to train muay thai in Bangkok

Watching a card usually makes people want to try it. Bangkok has gyms for every level, from hotel-style beginner classes to fight camps where active stadium fighters train. Names that come up regularly include **Petchyindee**, **Khongsittha** and **Yokkao**, and many smaller neighbourhood camps welcome beginners too.

A **drop-in group class typically costs around 400 to 800 THB**, with weekly and monthly packages bringing the per-session price down. Morning sessions start early, often around 7 am, to beat the heat, and evening sessions usually run from about 4 pm. Bring water, hand wraps if you have them, and expect to do a lot of skipping and pad work in your first session.

If you train for fitness more broadly, you might also enjoy [HYROX and fitness races in Bangkok](/blog/hyrox-fitness-races-bangkok-thailand).

## FAQ

### Which is better for tourists, Rajadamnern or Lumpinee?

Rajadamnern is easier for most visitors: it is central, near the old town, and has fights nearly every night. Lumpinee is worth the trip for a ONE Friday Fights card or a big title night, but it is further out in Bang Khen.

### How much are muay thai tickets in Bangkok?

Roughly 1,000 to 1,500 THB for the cheapest seats and around 2,000 to 4,500 THB for ringside at Rajadamnern. Premium seats for major ONE cards at Lumpinee can cost more. Always confirm on the official ticket page.

### What night is best to watch muay thai in Bangkok?

For a polished show, Saturday RWS at Rajadamnern or Friday ONE Friday Fights at Lumpinee. For a traditional atmosphere with live music and betting crowds, try a weeknight card such as Petchyindee or Kiatpetch.

### Can I buy tickets at the door?

Usually yes, at the box office, but big nights like RWS and headline ONE cards can sell out. Booking online a few days ahead is safer, and always buy from the official counter rather than touts.

### Is muay thai suitable for kids or first-timers?

Yes, many families attend, especially the modern shows. It is a full-contact sport, though, so expect knockouts and some blood. Ringside is the most intense spot.

### Can beginners train at a Bangkok muay thai gym?

Absolutely. Most gyms run mixed-level group classes and welcome drop-ins. Tell the trainer it is your first session and they will pace it accordingly.
