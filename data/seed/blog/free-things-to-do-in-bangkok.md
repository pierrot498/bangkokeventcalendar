There are more free things to do in Bangkok than most visitors expect. The headline sights, like the Grand Palace, charge foreigners a hefty fee, but the city's best parks, many of its art galleries, its neighbourhood temples and some of its most atmospheric streets cost nothing. Add a river ferry for the price of a snack and you can fill several days on a tiny budget.

Here is how to do it, area by area, with transport tips and honest caveats. For free events this week, like park concerts and gallery openings, check our [events listings](/events) and the [arts category](/events/category/arts).

## Parks and green spaces

### Lumpini Park

Bangkok's central park sits between Silom and Ratchadamri, next to **MRT Lumphini**, **MRT Silom** and **BTS Sala Daeng**. It is free, opens very early in the morning and stays open into the evening. Come at dawn to see tai chi groups, runners and outdoor aerobics, or late afternoon for paddle-boat views and the famous monitor lizards lounging by the lake. At **18:00** the national anthem plays and everyone stops for a moment; stand still with them.

In the cool season, the **Royal Bangkok Symphony Orchestra** has run free Sunday evening concerts in Lumpini. In 2026 the season ran from January to early March, starting at 17:30. Watch our calendar for next season's dates.

### Benjakitti Forest Park

The newer, wilder neighbour of Lumpini. Benjakitti mixes wetlands, a lake and an elevated **skywalk** that lets you walk above the treetops with skyscraper views. Entry is free and it is open daily from early morning to about 21:00. Use **MRT Queen Sirikit National Convention Centre exit 3**. The "Green Mile" walkway links it towards Lumpini, making a lovely long walk.

### More free parks

- **Chatuchak Park, Queen Sirikit Park and Rot Fai Park** around **MRT Chatuchak Park / BTS Mo Chit**, big lawns and cycle paths.
- **Chulalongkorn University Centenary Park** near Samyan, a modern green space built to handle floodwater.
- **Benchasiri Park** next to **BTS Phrom Phong**, small but handy in Sukhumvit.

Bangkok's parks are also where the free run clubs meet. See [running in Bangkok](/sports/running) or browse clubs on [runclubbangkok.com](https://runclubbangkok.com).

## Free museums and galleries

### Bangkok Art and Culture Centre (BACC)

The BACC is the best free culture stop in the city. Its spiral ramps lead to large contemporary exhibitions on the upper floors, with small galleries, bookshops and cafes lower down. Most exhibitions are free, though the odd special show may charge. It opens **Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 20:00**, and closes on Mondays. It connects by skywalk to **BTS National Stadium**, across from MBK.

### Commercial galleries

Most private galleries in Bangkok are free to visit. Clusters around **Charoen Krung**, **Sathorn**, and **Ratchada** host regular openings, often with free drinks on opening night. Our [art galleries and exhibitions guide](/blog/bangkok-art-galleries-exhibitions-guide) lists the best ones and how to find openings.

### Bank of Thailand Museum

Set in the historic **Bang Khun Phrom Palace** near the river, this museum covers Thai money and economic history and is free. Since the palace reopened in **July 2026**, visits run as **guided tours booked in advance**, mostly on weekends. Check the Bank of Thailand Learning Center's site before going.

## Temples that cost nothing (or almost nothing)

The big three charge foreigners: the **Grand Palace**, **Wat Pho** and **Wat Arun** each carry a fee in the hundreds of baht, and the **Golden Mount** (Wat Saket) is around **100 THB**. But hundreds of Bangkok temples are free to enter and see far fewer tourists.

- **Wat Mangkon Kamalawat** in Chinatown, the busy Chinese-Buddhist temple next to **MRT Wat Mangkon**, usually free and full of incense and colour.
- **Erawan Shrine** at Ratchaprasong, near **BTS Chit Lom**, where you can watch dancers performing for worshippers.
- **Neighbourhood temples** across Thonburi and the old town, where you can often wander the grounds freely.

**Temple etiquette:** cover shoulders and knees, take off shoes before entering halls, do not point your feet at Buddha images, and keep voices low. A small donation is appreciated but never required at free temples.

## Walks and neighbourhoods

### Chinatown (Yaowarat) walking route

Start at **MRT Wat Mangkon**, explore Wat Mangkon, then wander the lanes of **Sampeng** for wholesale shops and old Chinese pharmacies. At dusk, walk **Yaowarat Road** as the neon lights up and street food carts set up. Browsing is free; the food is cheap. Continue south to **Talad Noi** for street art, old shophouses and riverside views.

### The old town and Rattanakosin

Walk from **MRT Sanam Chai** past the outside walls of the Grand Palace, along the Sanam Luang open ground, and over to the Democracy Monument and Ratchadamnoen Avenue. Many of the best views are of buildings you do not need a ticket to admire from outside.

### Charoen Krung

The riverside road from Chinatown to Saphan Taksin mixes colonial-era buildings, galleries, coffee shops and street art. Weekends often bring free pop-up events.

## Almost free: river boats and ferries

The Chao Phraya River is the best cheap sightseeing ride in Bangkok.

- **Orange flag Chao Phraya Express:** a single trip is about **18 THB** at any distance (fares shift slightly with fuel prices). Board at **Sathorn pier**, below **BTS Saphan Taksin**, and ride north past Wat Arun to Tha Chang or beyond.
- **Cross-river ferries:** a handful of baht to hop between banks, for example from Tha Tien to Wat Arun.
- **Khlong Saen Saep canal boats:** fast, local and cheap, connecting the old town to Pratunam and Sukhumvit.

Skip the "Tourist boat" if you are counting baht; the orange flag covers most of the same piers for much less.

## Free events and seasonal moments

Bangkok's festivals are often free to watch. **Loy Krathong** brings riverside and park gatherings in November (see our [Loy Krathong 2026 guide](/blog/loy-krathong-2026-bangkok)), and the city runs free outdoor music seasons in parks at different points of the year. Malls stage free shows, holiday light displays and countdown stages, and many night markets are free to wander. Check the [calendar](/calendar) for what is on during your dates.

## Practical tips for a free Bangkok day

- **Go early.** Parks are coolest and most lively before 8:00.
- **Carry water and a refillable bottle.** Many malls and some parks have refill points.
- **Use the trains.** BTS and MRT fares run roughly **17 to 50 THB** per trip, far cheaper than taxis in traffic.
- **Dress for temples** even if you only plan to walk past; you may want to pop in.
- **Watch for scams.** Anyone telling you a famous temple is "closed today" and offering a tuk-tuk tour is a classic trick.
- **Use malls as rest stops.** Air conditioning, toilets and food courts are free or cheap.
- **Check Monday closures.** BACC and many galleries shut on Mondays.

## FAQ

### What are the best free things to do in Bangkok?

Lumpini and Benjakitti parks, the BACC, Chinatown at night, free temples like Wat Mangkon, and walks through Talad Noi and Charoen Krung.

### Is the BACC really free?

Yes, most exhibitions are free. It opens Tuesday to Sunday from 10:00 to 20:00 and is closed on Mondays.

### Which Bangkok temples are free to enter?

Most neighbourhood temples are free, as are Wat Mangkon in Chinatown and the Erawan Shrine. The Grand Palace, Wat Pho, Wat Arun and the Golden Mount charge foreign visitors.

### How much is the Chao Phraya Express boat?

The orange flag boat costs about 18 THB per trip, with small changes when fuel prices move. Cross-river ferries cost only a few baht.

### Are Bangkok parks safe and open at night?

Major parks like Lumpini and Benjakitti are open into the evening, to around 21:00, and are generally safe. They close overnight.

### Are there free concerts in Bangkok?

Yes. The Royal Bangkok Symphony Orchestra has held free Sunday concerts in Lumpini Park in the cool season, and the city runs other free park music events. Check our listings for current dates.
